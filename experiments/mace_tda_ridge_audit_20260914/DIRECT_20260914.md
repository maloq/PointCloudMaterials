# Direct topology agreement of frozen random and MLIP embeddings

The direct comparison fits nothing. It uses the verified original MLIP encoder
outputs and three random MACE initializations, without training a readout,
standardizing feature channels, projecting coordinates or selecting parameters.
All 4,608 original held-out neighborhoods are evaluated. Both Euclidean and
cosine embedding distances are reported; neither was selected using the results.

## Results

Each number averages the three separate H0/H1/H2 scores. Random MACE additionally
averages seeds 20260910–20260912; MLIP is one fixed original checkpoint.

| Encoder | Embedding distance | Global rank correlation | Within-frame rank correlation | Within-frame top-10 overlap |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Random MACE | Euclidean | 0.7451 | 0.3003 | 8.10% |
| Original MLIP | Euclidean | 0.8202 | 0.3531 | 8.67% |
| Random MACE | Cosine | 0.8169 | 0.3504 | 8.67% |
| Original MLIP | Cosine | 0.8466 | 0.3984 | 9.29% |

Within-frame searches contain 256 structures; chance top-10 overlap is 3.92%.
Global searches contain 4,608 structures; top-10 overlap is 1.29–1.45%, compared
with chance 0.217%. One should compare each scope against its own chance level;
their candidate-pool sizes differ. Shuffled correspondence gives correlations
near zero and overlap near the relevant chance level. No top-10 boundary ties
occurred in either the embedding or target distances.

The individual random seeds have within-frame cosine correlations 0.3253,
0.3690 and 0.3569, versus MLIP 0.3984. Their overlaps are 7.98%, 9.10% and 8.93%,
versus MLIP 9.29%. These are descriptive results for the retained models and
previously examined cohort, not uncertainty over all possible initializations.

MLIP's geometry agrees somewhat better with topology than random MACE's geometry
under both distances. Random MACE nevertheless has meaningful agreement without
any supervised readout. The large drop after restricting comparisons to one
frame shows why the global correlation alone is insufficient evidence of precise
local structural discrimination. Even within frames, overlap remains limited:
MLIP's cosine result corresponds to about 0.93 of ten neighbors matching on
average, versus 0.39 expected by chance. It is not 9.29% crystallization accuracy.

## How the direct metrics are calculated

For every pair of structures, Euclidean distance compares raw embedding
coordinates. Cosine distance is one minus their normalized dot product and
compares embedding directions. No dataset statistics enter either calculation.
The same pair gets three target distances: ordinary Euclidean distance in the
raw H0 block, H1 block and H2 block separately. This avoids learning a combined
target scale or assuming correspondence between embedding channels and TDA pixels.

**Spearman correlation** compares the rankings of embedding distances and target
distances. A value of 1 means identical pair-distance ordering, 0 means little
monotonic association, and -1 means reversed ordering. The global calculation uses
100,000 distinct unordered pairs chosen once with a fixed seed; each within-frame
calculation uses all 32,640 pairs. Within-frame scores average 18 frames, preserving
simulation/time/temperature context. Pairs share structures, so no ordinary
independent-pair p-values are used.

**Top-10 neighbor overlap** finds each structure's ten closest other structures
in embedding space and independently in each target block. The score is the
fraction of those ten neighbors shared by the two sets, averaged over queries.
All eligible structures are searched; the global pair sampling does not limit
nearest-neighbor candidates. Fixed k=10 is an analysis definition, with no search
for a more favorable k. Exact distance ties would use stable row order and are
reported; none occurred here.

## What ridge meant in the previous experiment

Ridge is a separate linear model mapping a 256D embedding z to a 144D descriptor:

`predicted_target = W z + b`.

It learns W and b from training examples by minimizing

`sum(training squared prediction errors) + alpha * sum(W²)`.

The second term is the **ridge penalty**. Larger alpha discourages large readout
weights, often making predictions less sensitive to weak or redundant feature
directions. Alpha 1 was the original fixed setting. Choosing alpha from validation
was the later sensitivity analysis. Even alpha 0 would still fit a supervised
linear readout. None of these readout operations occurs in the direct experiment.

The previous reported error was the average of three normalized block MSEs:

`balanced_MSE = (MSE_H0 / s_H0² + MSE_H1 / s_H1² + MSE_H2 / s_H2²) / 3`.

Each MSE averages prediction errors over test structures and coordinates in that
block. Each scale is the square root of the mean training coordinate variance
plus a common squared floor; the floor is 5% of the largest training block standard
deviation. The retained scales are 0.05992898, 0.01308863 and 0.00892571. Equal
block weights prevent the 64D blocks automatically outweighing 16D H0. This is a
dimensionless reconstruction error; it is neither classification accuracy nor
necessarily 1 minus an R² score. The direct experiment produces no descriptor
predictions and therefore does not use this MSE.

## Which TDA features are used

The targets are calculated after full-cell Lee2003 MEAM FIRE energy relaxation,
keeping the same 80 atom identities selected in the hot input. Thus embeddings
describe the original noisy anchor and the target describes its relaxed geometry.
This is not a future trajectory or a crystal/noncrystal label.

The original checksummed `persistence_image` implementation builds an alpha complex.
Conceptually, balls grow around atoms: components merge, loops appear and close,
and cavities appear and fill. Persistence records their birth and death radii.
The implementation converts squared alpha-filtration values to radii in Angstrom,
removes infinite-death intervals, and excludes deaths above 3.5 Angstrom.

| Block | Meaning | Actual descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| H0, 16 values | Connected-component merging | Smooth death-radius curve at 16 points from 0.7 to 2.5 Angstrom; Gaussian width 0.10 Angstrom |
| H1, 64 values | Loops/tunnels | Flattened 8x8 birth-radius / lifetime Gaussian surface, weighted by lifetime |
| H2, 64 values | Cavities | Same 8x8 construction for H2 persistence intervals |

The H1/H2 birth grid spans 0.7–3.0 Angstrom and the lifetime grid spans
0.025–1.1 Angstrom; smoothing widths are 0.15 and 0.09 Angstrom respectively.
Contributions are summed and divided by 79. The 144 values are smooth continuous
descriptors, not raw persistence diagrams, integer Betti counts or phase labels.
