# What the overnight MACE experiments established — September 11, 2026

**Multiple observations help recover relaxed topology, but learned temporal
attention has not beaten mean pooling consistently. Checkpoint selection is a
major confounder in the trained TDA-head results.** The best evaluated
representation is the trainable five-frame mean encoder with a training-only
ridge readout. This supports a modest denoising benefit for this descriptor;
it does not establish reconstruction of the relaxed atomic structure.

All twelve overnight fits completed 24 epochs and their standard post-training
analysis. Together with the twelve earlier MEAM fits, this gives eight variants
with three seeds each. The full Al/Mg/Ta/Ti checkpoint is an additional reference.
No training remains active in this campaign.

The [September 14 independent retest](../mace_tda_ridge_audit_20260914/README.md)
reproduced the single-frame VICReg/TDA ridge comparison with fresh inference and
verified the intended supervision paths. It distinguishes the 128D projector
used here from the 256D encoder used in forecasting and reports both separately.

The final overnight collector failed because it read its own earlier
`comparison/metrics.json` as a model report. The maintained collector now
discovers reports through their `source.json` provenance files, and both legacy
and flat-layout repeated-collection tests pass. All 24 fits were successfully
reaggregated without retraining or changing checkpoint selection.

## Comparable results

All numbers below use the same six held-out MEAM sources and 4,608 anchor
neighborhoods. Each row is the mean across seeds 20260910/20260911/20260912;
± is the population standard deviation across those three seeds, matching the
standard collector. Lower balanced MSE is better. H0/H1/H2 receive equal weight
after scaling fitted on training sources only.

| Encoder / objective | Trained TDA-head test MSE | Test MSE with the same ridge procedure |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Single frame, VICReg only | No TDA head | 0.032914 ± 0.000967 |
| Single frame, VICReg + PCA32 TDA | 0.091818 ± 0.024869 | 0.033352 ± 0.000949 |
| Single frame, VICReg + balanced TDA | 0.056853 ± 0.007362 | 0.032813 ± 0.001004 |
| Five-frame mean | 0.076407 ± 0.021371 | **0.029632 ± 0.000991** |
| Pooled temporal transformer | 0.359791 ± 0.449792 | 0.030912 ± 0.002492 |
| Anchor plus temporal residual | 0.063844 ± 0.021265 | 0.031190 ± 0.000385 |
| Atom-level matched anchor control | 0.058160 ± 0.009631 | 0.032833 ± 0.000188 |
| Atom-level temporal attention | 0.073185 ± 0.013753 | 0.030542 ± 0.000708 |

The ridge column is the fairer comparison of retained representations: each
model gets the same standardized linear readout, fitted on training sources
with fixed alpha 1. It is a separate decoder fitted after encoder training;
it is not the jointly trained TDA head. In the automatic summary, VICReg-only
uses its ridge result as the primary prediction, so that entry must not be
mistaken for a trained-head score.

Changing the single-frame target from whitened PCA32 to balanced H0/H1/H2
improved trained-head error by 38.08%, but improved ridge error by only 1.62%.
Most of that apparent benefit concerns target/readout training rather than a
large improvement in the retained encoder's linearly recoverable information.

The single-frame balanced model already has mean within-frame R² **0.8494**
with ridge; mean pooling reaches **0.8643**. A single noisy neighborhood thus
contains substantial information about this particular relaxed descriptor.
The history gain is useful but considerably smaller than the total information
already recoverable from one observation. The training-mean baseline has test
balanced MSE 0.839816, compared with 0.029632 for mean pooling plus ridge.

The lowest validation ridge error among the evaluated retained checkpoints is
[mean pooling, seed 20260912](../../output/mace/mean-blocks-seed20260912/index.html):
validation **0.023978**, test **0.028333**. This is a useful individual reference;
the three-seed result above remains the architecture comparison.

## How strong is the benefit from history?

These are paired error reductions using the ridge readouts. Positive values
favor the candidate. Intervals resample the six whole sources after averaging
errors over the three seeds.

| Candidate versus reference | Error reduction | 95% source-bootstrap interval |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mean pooling versus single frame | **9.69%** | **[1.19%, 15.43%]** |
| Atom temporal versus matched atom anchor | **6.98%** | **[2.60%, 10.75%]** |
| Pooled transformer versus mean pooling | −4.32% | [−8.54%, 0.45%] |
| Temporal residual versus single frame | 4.94% | [−2.52%, 10.81%] |

Mean pooling and atom temporal each improve on five of the six test sources.
The atom comparison is especially informative because both models have the
same fusion capacity. Its improvement cannot be attributed solely to adding
that network. Conversely, neither pooled attention nor residual correction
has a clear advantage over its declared reference in this three-seed result.

Mean pooling here is part of training: gradients pass through the shared MACE
backbone and the mean operation. The frame representations can adapt to the
pooling operation. Its success is an empirical finding about this jointly
trained encoder.

The intervals are conditional on these three fitted seeds; they do not include
uncertainty from drawing new optimization seeds. Six sources are few, and this
test cohort was already examined in the frozen-MACE study. These are exploratory
follow-up results, not an untouched confirmation set.

## What information is being recovered?

The improvement is concentrated in H0. The repository descriptor encodes H0
as a smoothed death-radius curve; H1/H2 are lifetime-weighted persistence images.

| Ridge error reduction | H0 | H1 | H2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mean versus single frame | **20.41%** | 7.88% | 0.42% |
| Atom temporal versus matched anchor | **16.76%** | 5.07% | −1.46% |

This is consistent with averaging out fluctuations in local connectivity and
distance structure. It is much weaker evidence for recovering additional loop
or cavity information. The experiments do not establish improved identification
of dislocations, grain boundaries or symmetry breaking: those require independent
structural labels or an explicit defect benchmark.

Mean-pooling gains also depend on temperature: **13.80% at 400 K**, **0.44% at
450 K**, and **15.33% at 510 K** relative to the single-frame model. Each
temperature has only two held-out sources, so this pattern should not be
interpreted as a resolved physical temperature law.

The existing interventions distinguish the benefit of multiple observations
from sensitivity to their order:

| Encoder | Ridge MSE with real history | Repeated anchor | Reversed past, anchor fixed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mean | 0.029632 | 0.038298 | 0.029632 |
| Pooled transformer | 0.030912 | 0.039353 | 0.031901 |
| Residual | 0.031190 | 0.036834 | 0.031335 |
| Atom temporal | 0.030542 | 0.065145 | 0.030956 |

Replacing observations with copies of the anchor worsens mean-pooling error by
29.25% and atom-temporal error by 113.29%. Reversing the past changes atom-temporal
error by only 1.36% and pooled-transformer error by 3.20%; mean pooling is
invariant as expected. This supports the value of multiple observations more
strongly than the value of their exact ordering in the present protocol.
Repeated-anchor inference is a distribution shift for a history-trained model;
its degradation is not an unbiased estimate of the attainable single-frame
error. The separately trained atom-anchor control supplies that comparison.

## Why the trained-head results look much worse

The checkpoint rule minimizes combined validation VICReg + TDA loss. It does
not minimize validation topology error. W&B histories show a mismatch in
**15 of the 18 balanced-TDA fits** inspected below.

| Variant | Validation TDA MSE at retained checkpoint | Lowest logged validation TDA MSE during training |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Single frame | 0.05523 | 0.03389 |
| Mean | 0.07075 | 0.03601 |
| Pooled transformer | 0.37813 | 0.03804 |
| Residual | 0.06416 | 0.03646 |
| Atom anchor | 0.05888 | 0.03422 |
| Atom temporal | 0.07552 | 0.04151 |

These are averages of validation logs, not newly measured test scores.
They show that the retained checkpoints often understate what the heads learned
later. They do not establish what the test ranking would be under a different
selection rule. Even the best logged validation head errors do not give a
consistent advantage to temporal attention over the single-frame controls.

The clearest example is
[transformer seed 20260912](https://wandb.ai/teshbek/PointCloudMaterials/runs/t8prho3v):

| Logged validation quantity | Epoch 0, retained | Epoch 23, final |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Combined loss | **22.7889** | 25.4007 |
| VICReg contribution | 21.7256 | 25.3577 |
| Balanced TDA MSE | 1.06324 | **0.04297** |

Validation covariance penalties spiked during training; the spatial covariance
term reached about 984 at epoch 9. The combined loss never beat epoch 0, even
after topology prediction recovered. The retained epoch-0 checkpoint has test
head MSE **0.995893**, worse than the training-mean baseline, but its ridge MSE
is **0.034407**. This is not evidence that the representation contains no
topology information. The other two transformer seeds have head test MSE
0.041575 and 0.041904; dropping the third seed would conceal a failure of the
actual training-and-selection procedure.

The later epoch-23 weights were not retained locally under the lean checkpoint
policy. Their validation score survives in W&B, but their test score is unknown;
it cannot be substituted into the comparison. For any later training, retaining
one checkpoint per scientific criterion—best topology and best VICReg—would
avoid this loss of evidence while still keeping storage small.

Across all retained models, ridge is also better than the jointly trained head.
That identifies readout fitting and checkpoint selection as actionable
bottlenecks. Representation drift, BatchNorm evaluation behavior and the relative
loss scales are plausible contributors; the present logs do not isolate a
specific numerical or normalization bug. The observed selection mismatch is
directly measured and should be resolved before attributing the head failures
to missing geometric information or increasing model complexity.

## What to keep from this campaign

- **Reference representation:** five-frame mean MACE with the standard ridge
  evaluation. It has the best average probe result and lowest validation probe
  error among the retained checkpoints.
- **Efficient baseline:** single-frame MACE + VICReg. Its ridge MSE 0.032914
  is almost identical to single-frame VICReg + TDA at 0.032813; adding the TDA
  objective did not materially improve this evaluated representation.
- **Useful evidence:** the matched atom-level comparison supports extra
  information in multiple snapshots, although its learned fusion is not a
  better overall representation than mean pooling here.
- **Unresolved:** whether a topology-selected, properly fitted temporal head
  improves held-out performance. Current retained-head rankings are confounded
  by selection. No new fit was launched for this review.

The full Al/Mg/Ta/Ti checkpoint has MEAM ridge test MSE **0.034143** and therefore
transfers reasonably to this evaluation. It is one model trained on a different
dataset and update budget, so this is not a controlled test of adding Ta/Ti or
mixing potentials. The comparison does not establish Ta/Ti test performance.

UMAP/t-SNE and structural representatives are available in the
[flat gallery](../../output/mace/index.html). Single-frame main plots use the
large static-Al cohort; history main plots use the 4,608 MEAM test histories.
Their silhouettes and visual separation are not directly comparable across
those cohorts. All topology numbers in this report use the common MEAM stage.
There is no relaxed-input oracle or coordinate-denoising evaluation in this
24-fit matrix, so it does not resolve the remaining uncertainty about exact
relaxation or the maximum accuracy of these particular TDA targets.
