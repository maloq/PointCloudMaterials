# What changes between the two Al potentials?

Measured 2026-09-10 on the completed matched-potential audit. The strongest
conclusion is that these potentials give different local minima and different
local packing on the same starting structures. Much of the persistence-image
difference is a repeatable mean shift. Correcting that shift does not recover
the neighborhood-specific variation.

## What the two files represent

`Lee2003_MEAM` is the second-nearest-neighbor modified embedded-atom model of
Lee, Shim and Baskes (2003). `Al1_EAM_FS` is the Mendelev, Kramer, Becker and
Asta (2008) Al model. The local Al1 file was checked against the official
LAMMPS `patch_2Sep2026/potentials/Al_mm.eam.fs`: every non-comment token is
identical. The apparent 2007 date and “will be published” in its header do not
make it a different model. [Identity check](../../output/mace_al_denoising_20260910/available_mixed/potential_audit/deeper_analysis/potential_identity.json).
NIST also identifies Al1 and Al_mm as the same model apart from comments.
[NIST Al1 entry](https://www.ctcms.nist.gov/potentials/entry/2008--Mendelev-M-I-Kramer-M-J-Becker-C-A-Asta-M--Al/).

Both are classical, many-body metal potentials, with a pair-energy term and
an embedding-energy term. EAM/FS uses radial neighbor-density contributions;
MEAM adds angular dependence and many-body screening. It is incorrect to
describe EAM as a pair-only potential, or to conclude that the more complicated
functional form is automatically more accurate.
[LAMMPS EAM](https://docs.lammps.org/pair_eam.html),
[LAMMPS MEAM](https://docs.lammps.org/pair_meam.html).

| File property | Lee2003 MEAM | Mendelev Al1 EAM/FS |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Reference crystal | FCC Al | FCC Al |
| Reference lattice parameter recorded in file | 4.044651 Å | 4.045270 Å |
| Interaction cutoff | 4.5 Å | 6.5 Å |
| Explicit angular density / screening | Yes | No MEAM angular/screening terms |

The reference lattice parameters differ by only 0.015%. They are file
parameters, not an independent equation-of-state measurement in this audit.
Different cutoffs, fitted functions and angular/screening treatment can all
contribute to the measured differences; this comparison cannot isolate the
contribution of one term.

The Lee paper addresses FCC bulk, point-defect, planar-defect and thermal
properties. The Mendelev paper specifically emphasizes deeply undercooled
liquids, liquid diffraction data and crystallization kinetics in addition to
crystalline properties. Its authors show why similar equilibrium properties
need not imply similar rapid-quench kinetics. This gives a reason to expect
differences in disordered environments; it does not establish which model is
more accurate for our samples.
[Lee et al., 2003](https://www.ctcms.nist.gov/potentials/entry/2003--Lee-B-J-Shim-J-H-Baskes-M-I--Al/),
[Mendelev et al., 2008](https://www.nist.gov/publications/analysis-semi-empirical-interatomic-potentials-appropriate-simulation-crystalline-and).

## What was held fixed

Nine independent MEAM-generated source cells: three each at 400, 450 and
510 K, one early anchor at 30 ps per source. Every cell has 70,304 Al atoms.
Both minimizations start with exactly the same full periodic positions, box,
identities and composition. Both use the same validated LAMMPS GPU binary,
FIRE, initial timestep 0.001 ps and maximum-force requirement 0.01 eV/Å.
There is no box relaxation. The same 256 centers and the same 80 identities
per center define both sets of TDA targets: 2,304 paired patches altogether.
The model and its prediction are also identical for the two labels.

Thus the target comparison is not confounded by the 650-versus-400 K or
different-history-cadence differences in the earlier mixed training data.
It does remain conditional on MEAM-generated starting states. It does not
compare two independently equilibrated liquids generated by their own potentials.

The quenched states are fixed-volume inherent structures. Energy minimization
does not determine the most probable finite-temperature structure or prove that
a global energy minimum was reached.

## The force fields agree on much of the initial motion, but not on the endpoint

Single-point calculations evaluated both force fields on the original hot
positions and both exact saved relaxed snapshots; no atoms were moved.
Mean atomic force-direction cosine on the hot states is **0.882**. Only
**1.36%** of atoms have opposing force directions. There is substantial initial
agreement, although a positive cosine is not equality of forces or trajectories.

| Force evaluator | RMS force on MEAM-relaxed state | RMS force on EAM-relaxed state |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| MEAM | 0.000309 eV/Å | 0.1814 eV/Å |
| EAM | 0.2272 eV/Å | 0.000354 eV/Å |

These are RMS vector magnitudes, averaged over source cells. The original
maximum-component convergence requirement was independently reproduced for
every own-potential state. Each endpoint remains far from force equilibrium
under the other potential.

Within the MEAM energy function, the EAM endpoint is **31.4 meV/atom higher**
than the MEAM endpoint. Within EAM, the MEAM endpoint is **45.4 meV/atom higher**
than the EAM endpoint. Both preferences hold in all nine sources. These are
within-potential energy comparisons; comparing absolute energies from different
potentials would not establish physical accuracy. They also do not establish
that the endpoints are global minima.

Identical volume does not mean identical pressure. With zero velocities, the
own-potential relaxed virial pressures average -1.91 GPa for MEAM and
-1.44 GPa for EAM. Fixed-cell labels are therefore not zero-pressure equilibria.

## What changes structurally, and what remains similar

| Measurement | MEAM relaxation | EAM relaxation |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mean atom displacement from common hot input | 0.417 Å | 0.931 Å |
| Mean nearest-neighbor distance | 2.707 Å | 2.728 Å |
| Mean distance to first 12 neighbors | 2.972 Å | 2.952 Å |
| Mean within-patch spread of those 12 distances | 0.285 Å | 0.192 Å |
| Mean neighbors within 3.5 Å | 10.80 | 11.90 |
| Mean bond-order q6 at sampled centers | 0.355 | 0.452 |
| PTM FCC atoms | 0.183% | 2.209% |
| PTM HCP atoms | 0.434% | 2.166% |
| PTM icosahedral atoms | 0.0014% | 0.735% |
| PTM Other / unclassified | 99.382% | 94.888% |

PTM was computed on complete periodic cells, with FCC/HCP/BCC/ICO templates and
RMSD cutoff 0.1. “Other” means no sufficiently close template match, not a
thermodynamic phase label. These remain predominantly disordered configurations;
the larger EAM motif fractions do not establish a bulk crystal or a grain boundary.
[PTM definition and cutoff](https://docs.ovito.org/reference/pipelines/modifiers/polyhedral_template_matching.html).

The apparent small change in the first-shell mean hides a **32.5% reduction in
its distance spread**. The first neighbor moves outward on average while the
outer part of the first 12 moves inward. This is a change in shell shape, not
a uniform expansion/contraction. The RDF also changes around its second shell.

Across endpoints, **82.2% of the 12 nearest-neighbor identities** are retained;
the overlap for the 79 neighbors of each 80-atom patch is **86.2%**. Local
membership is largely retained but not identical. The TDA comparison itself
keeps the original membership fixed, avoiding an extraction-induced mismatch.
Mean atom displacement between endpoints is **0.956 Å**. After fitting a
translation, rotation and isotropic scale to each identity-aligned patch,
the residual RMS displacement still averages **0.968 Å**. These two numbers
use different reductions (mean atomic distance versus per-patch RMS), so they
should not be interpreted as a before/after improvement ratio. The fitted
scale averages 0.9972, supporting that rigid motion and simple scaling do not
explain the rearrangement.

The same pattern occurs at all three starting temperatures:

| Starting temperature | MEAM-to-EAM mean displacement | First-12 identity overlap | Balanced TDA target distance |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 400 K | 0.932 Å | 82.75% | 0.862 |
| 450 K | 0.938 Å | 82.55% | 0.933 |
| 510 K | 0.998 Å | 81.33% | 1.072 |

Each row averages three sources. This describes the selected early frames;
it is not a general law for temperature dependence or nucleation rates.

## Which topological measurements differ?

The descriptor uses the same alpha-complex persistence code, in physical
radius units (Å), with finite deaths beyond 3.5 Å excluded. It summarizes
connectivity (H0), loops (H1) and cavities (H2) over a filtration. The final
filled alpha complex of a finite point cloud is not itself the discriminative
quantity; the birth/death scales are.

| Per-patch persistence statistic | MEAM | EAM |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Finite H0 intervals | 79 | 79 |
| Mean H0 merge radius | 1.3665 Å | 1.3794 Å |
| Finite H1 intervals | 275.70 | 276.86 |
| Mean H1 lifetime | 0.1531 Å | 0.1603 Å |
| Finite H2 intervals | 180.67 | 198.03 |
| Total H2 persistence | 9.499 Å | 11.229 Å |
| H2 intervals with lifetime >0.05 Å | 55.58 | 93.27 |
| H2 intervals with lifetime >0.10 Å | 24.71 | 19.33 |

The H0 count is fixed by the common 80-point cardinality. H1 counts are nearly
unchanged. Nevertheless, persistence lengths and H2 lifetime distribution
change substantially. In particular, EAM has more medium-lived H2 features
but fewer above 0.10 Å; “more cavities” without stating the lifetime criterion
would be misleading. These features are not a count of vacancies or lattice defects.

## Why the 9× model-error ratio needs qualification

The balanced squared distance between the two target descriptors is 0.9558.
An exact mean-plus-residual decomposition attributes **81.0%** of this to a
global per-pixel mean shift. By block, the global-mean fractions are 84.2%
(H0), 76.9% (H1) and 81.7% (H2). Allowing a separate mean for each source changes
these only slightly, to 85.0%, 77.4% and 81.9%.

A post-hoc leave-one-source-out check fits a constant EAM-minus-MEAM target
offset using eight source cells, then evaluates the ninth:

| Quantity | Balanced MSE |
| --- | ---: |
| MEAM targets used directly as predictions of EAM targets | 0.9558 |
| Same after an offset fitted on the other eight sources | 0.1828 |
| Fixed network versus MEAM targets | 0.0936 |
| Fixed network versus EAM targets | 0.8492 |
| Network plus offset, versus held-out EAM targets | 0.1316 |
| Constant EAM descriptor fitted on the other eight sources | 0.1306 |
| Constant MEAM descriptor fitted on the other eight sources | 0.1095 |

Thus **84.5% of the EAM prediction MSE is removable by a constant calibration**
in these cases. The earlier 9× error ratio is not evidence of nine times more
physical disorder or nine times less recoverable structural information.

But calibration is not sufficient for local topology learning. After subtracting
each source's per-pixel mean, paired descriptor correlations are only **0.195,
0.178 and 0.189** for H0/H1/H2. The corrected network performs about the same
as a constant EAM descriptor. These early disordered frames reveal limited
prediction of neighborhood-specific structure, even where average calibrated
error looks small. The correlations describe this descriptor and this matched
dataset, not a universal impossibility of nonlinear transfer.

Distance normalization did not fix the discrepancy. Rescaling both patches to
a first-12 mean distance of 2.86 Å increased balanced descriptor distance to
2.316. Rescaling only EAM to match the paired MEAM first-shell mean gave 2.958.
The descriptor uses fixed grids in Å, so these controls also move features
relative to the grid; the numerical increases should not be read as physical
distance measures. Together with the shell-width and alignment measurements,
they reject a simple first-shell scale correction for these labels.

Float16 storage of centered patch coordinates contributes only **5.9e-6**
balanced squared descriptor error, versus 0.956 for the potential difference.
This uses exact relaxed-dump coordinates at the first 16 recorded centers in
each source, recomputed through the same TDA function. It isolates local cache
quantization, not the effect of quantizing the common original trajectory before
either minimization. Existing full-cell CPU/GPU checks also agree near machine
precision. The measured difference is not explained by local float16 error or
the validated accelerated force implementation.

## Consequences for the encoder experiment

1. Specify which potential defines the relaxed target. Pooling these targets
   without identifying the potential creates different answers to the same
   geometry. Temporal observations can inform denoising within a landscape;
   they do not by themselves choose our definition of that landscape.
2. To combine existing labels, provide target-potential identity explicitly,
   for example through a shared encoder with separate topology heads. Mean
   offsets are useful baselines, but the weak local correspondence argues
   against assuming that an offset makes the targets equivalent.
3. To test temporal denoising cleanly, use one target potential and compare
   matched atom-anchor and temporal models on independent source trajectories,
   with source-centered metrics and a constant-descriptor baseline.
4. To test transfer between potentials, generate/evaluate both directions and
   include later crystalline/defective structures. This audit cannot decide
   which potential best predicts real Al defects, equilibrium phase stability,
   melting, diffusion or nucleation kinetics. That requires corresponding
   physical benchmarks, not choosing whichever target this network predicts best.

The original paired p=0.00390625 remains valid for its prespecified prediction-
error comparison. The diagnostics and cross-validated calibration here are
post-hoc explanations; no new significance claim is made from treating the
2,304 overlapping patches as independent replicates.

## Reproduction and artifacts

The new Python file is an experiment-specific reproduction recipe, not a new
maintained runner. It reads existing completed cases and evaluates static
forces; it performs no new MD integration, relaxation or encoder training.
The plot, per-source arrays, generated LAMMPS inputs/logs/force dumps and JSON
metrics are disposable derived results under the existing output directory.
This Markdown file is a versioned experiment record.

```bash
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 \
OVITO_THREAD_COUNT=1 PYTHONPATH=. conda run --no-capture-output -n pointnet \
  python experiments/mace_al_denoising_20260910/analyze_potential_differences.py
```

The force evaluations use the GPU binary and potential paths in
`potential_audit.json`; run inside a compatible allocated GPU environment.
The analysis verified existing cache checksums, atom ordering, neighborhood
identity, RDF query extent and all 18 own-potential force-convergence conditions.
